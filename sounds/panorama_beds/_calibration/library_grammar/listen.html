Testing whether the canonical acoustic-forward grammar for library/study produces authentic library/study hall audio instead of cafe/restaurant. Non-deterministic — each take is a different random seed of the same prompt. 12 seconds each (short = cheap ideation pass), prompt_influence: 0.3.

Large old stone library in daylight, listener seated between shelves, soft page rustle and cloth movement, very distant hushed study presence, sparse wood chair creaks, restrained natural reverb, calm and civilized, no close speech, no music, seamless ambient loop

What to listen for

  • Authorial fit: does it sound like a quiet old library? Stone/wood room, paper texture, restrained presence? Not a cafe, not a restaurant, not a kitchen.
  • Event density: sparse and irregular — no more than 1-2 audible events in 12 seconds. Too dense = reads as "people in a room" → cafe.
  • Reverb character: restrained but present — you should feel the stone room, not hear a dry recording booth.
  • Variance across takes: all 3 should read as "library" in different moods. If they're all cafe-ish, the grammar itself is failing. If 1 works and 2 don't, the grammar is close but needs a re-roll budget.

Outcome gates

  • Pass: ≥2 of 3 takes sound like a library → grammar locked for all library/study scenes (grand_library, study_benches, italian_study_1–6, ramchal_study, deans_study, brooklyn_beit_midrash, torah_study_hall, and the other library/study members)
